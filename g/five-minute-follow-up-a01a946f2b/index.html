The 5-Minute Follow-Up Kit: let AI write every follow-up so no lead goes cold

Your leads aren't saying no. Most of them just stop hearing from you. I've spent 20 years in lead generation, and one thing holds up almost everywhere: whoever answers first usually wins.

You don't need to hire someone to fix that. You need AI to write every follow-up before the lead ever comes in. In this kit, Claude writes three things for you: the instant reply that goes out the second someone fills out your form, the next seven days of follow-up, and the win-back text for old leads who never booked. Load them into your CRM once and they run every time.

If you can copy and paste, you can do all of it. Claude is the main tool here, and every prompt works the same in ChatGPT.

1

Step 1: Time how fast you answer today

Before you change anything, find out where you stand. Grab your phone and fill out your own contact form, the same one a new lead would use. Then start a timer.

  • How long until you hear back?
  • Was it a real message, or just an automatic "we got your request"?
  • Did it ask you anything, or leave you to make the next move?

Write the time down. Most owners are surprised by the number. That gap is where leads go cold, and it's what the rest of this kit closes.

What you need for the rest: Claude (the free plan works), a CRM or any tool that can send texts and emails automatically (whatever you already use), and a list of your past leads with names, contact details, the date they came in and whether they booked. No CRM yet? You can still use every message. Send them by hand until you set one up.

2

Step 2: Teach Claude your business (once)

This is what keeps the messages from sounding like a robot wrote them. Start a new chat, paste this in and fill in the brackets.

Copy this inI run a business called [business name]. We provide [your service] for [who your customers are] in [your area, or "online"]. Customers usually come to us because [the main problem they have]. The next step I want every lead to take is [book a call / book an estimate / reply to a text]. My booking link is [link]. How we talk to customers: [friendly and casual / direct and professional / warm but short]. We never use hype, pressure or fake urgency. Here are two or three real messages I've sent customers, so you can match my voice: 1. [paste a real message] 2. [paste a real message] Things I can honestly say about my business: [real facts only: years in business, what's included, service area, guarantees you actually offer] Reply with "Ready" and a one-sentence summary of my business so I can check you understood.

Read the summary. If anything is wrong, correct it. Keep this same chat open for every step below so Claude remembers it all. In ChatGPT, do the same in one chat thread.

3

Step 3: Write the instant reply

This text goes out the moment someone fills out your form. Two lines, one easy question. The question matters: it gives the lead something simple to answer, and a reply starts a conversation.

Copy this inWrite a text message that goes out automatically the moment someone fills out my contact form asking about my service. Rules: - Two lines at most. - Friendly and plain. No hype. - Greet them by first name using the placeholder {{first_name}}. - Say who we are in a few words. - End with one easy question they can answer in a few words. - No link in this first message. Give me five different versions so I can pick the one that sounds most like me.

Pick the one that sounds most like you and change any word you wouldn't say out loud. In your CRM, set up an automation (some tools call it a workflow) with the trigger "form submitted" and the action "send text." Send a test to your own phone and check that the first name fills in correctly.

4

Step 4: Write the 7-day follow-up

Most people send one message and quit. This step writes the next seven days, so you never have to remember who to chase.

Copy this inNow write a 7-day follow-up sequence for leads who got my first text but haven't replied or booked. Rules: - A mix of short texts and short emails. Texts under 300 characters. Emails under 100 words. - Each message gives one new reason to talk. Ideas: a question customers often ask, a simple tip about their problem, how our process works, the kind of job we handle most (in general terms, no made-up names or numbers), an offer to answer questions. - Never use the same reason twice. - No guilt, no fake deadlines, and no "just checking in" or "circling back." - Each message ends with one easy question or one clear next step. - Use {{first_name}} as the placeholder. - Label each message with its day (Day 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7) and its channel (text or email). Give every email a subject line. - Only use facts from my business details. If a message needs a fact I haven't given you, write [ADD REAL DETAIL] instead. Give me the full sequence.

Read every message out loud. If one doesn't sound like you, tell Claude, for example: "Make Day 3 shorter and more casual." Then load each message into your CRM as a timed step.

Add a stop rule. The sequence must stop the moment a lead replies or books. Nobody should get a follow-up after they've already answered. It looks careless, and it's the most common setup mistake.

5

Step 5: Wake up the leads who never booked

You already paid for these leads, with ad money or your own time. Some of them just got busy. One short text can start the conversation again.

In your CRM, filter for leads from the last 90 days who never booked. Export their first names and phone numbers. Then ask Claude for the message.

Copy this inWrite a win-back text for leads from the last 90 days who asked about my service but never booked. Rules: - One or two short sentences. - Sound like a real person, not a campaign. - Use {{first_name}}. - Ask a simple yes or no question, based on: "Hey, are you still looking for help with this?" - No discount, no pressure, no fake deadline. - Make it easy for them to say "no thanks" without feeling bad. Give me five versions, from most casual to most professional.

Pick one and send it to a small group first, about 10 to 20 people. See how they respond. If it goes well, send it to the rest.

Important: only text people who gave you permission to contact them, and include a way to opt out as your local rules require. Not sure about the rules where you are? Ask your CRM provider or a local professional before you send.

6

Step 6: Get your replies ready

When leads answer, you want to answer back fast. Have Claude write your saved replies now, so a busy day doesn't slow you down.

Copy this inHere are replies leads might send after my follow-up texts. For each one, write a short, helpful response I can send. Keep each under 40 words and end with a next step. 1. "How much does it cost?" 2. "Not right now." 3. "Can you call me?" 4. "I already found someone." 5. "What does that include?" 6. [a question your customers ask a lot] Use my business details from earlier. Don't make up prices. Where a price is needed, write [PRICE] so I can fill it in.

Save these as saved replies (sometimes called snippets or templates) in your CRM or phone.

A quick example. Here's how this could look for a made-up house-cleaning company called Clearwater Cleaning.

  • Instant reply: "Hi {{first_name}}, thanks for reaching out to Clearwater Cleaning. Is this for a one-time clean or regular visits?"
  • Day 2 text: "Hi {{first_name}}, a lot of our customers start with one deep clean and then decide if they want regular visits. Would that be a good place to start?"
  • Day 5 email (subject: What a first clean looks like): a three-sentence walk-through of the first visit, ending with "Want me to send a few open times this week?"
  • Win-back text, 90 days later: "Hey {{first_name}}, are you still looking for help with cleaning? No worries if not."

Every message is short, makes one point and ends with one easy question. That's the pattern to copy.

7

Step 7: Turn it on, then check it weekly

  1. Run Step 1 again: fill out your own form and time the reply. It should now arrive in seconds.
  2. Fill out the form a second time with a test name and don't reply. Make sure the Day 1 follow-up arrives, then reply and make sure the sequence stops.
  3. Once a week, look at which messages get answers and which get silence. Paste what you see into Claude.
Copy this inHere's how my follow-up messages did this week. I'm only sharing what I can see in my CRM, not guesses. [paste each message and how many replies or bookings it got] Tell me which message to rewrite first and give me two new versions of it. Keep the same rules as before.

Common mistakes:

  • Skipping Step 2. Without your business details, the messages come out generic.
  • Asking two questions in one message. One message, one question.
  • Forgetting the stop rule.
  • Letting AI invent customer names, results, prices or deadlines. Use placeholders and fill them with real facts.
  • Blasting the whole old list at once. Test a small group first.
  • Setting it up and never looking again.

Short on time today? Do Steps 2, 3 and 5. That's one instant reply and one win-back text, and it takes about ten minutes. Build the 7-day follow-up tomorrow.
