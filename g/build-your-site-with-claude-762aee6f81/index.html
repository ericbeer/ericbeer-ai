Build Your Site With Claude: plan, write, build and publish your business website with AI

You don't need to pay someone every time your website needs a change. Claude can plan the pages, write the copy, build the site and help you put it live. You stay in charge of what it says.

I built ericbeer.ai this way. It's a simple site: a home page, a free-guide library, a page for each guide, a waitlist and a contact page. All of it is generated from one data file, it's hosted free on GitHub Pages with my own domain, and the sign-up forms send people straight into my CRM (GoHighLevel). When I make a new guide, I add one entry to that file and it shows up. No rebuild.

The trick isn't the code. It's telling AI the one job your site has before it builds anything. Below are the exact prompts, in order. Use them in the same Claude chat. ChatGPT works the same for the planning and writing steps. There are a few one-time setup steps (an account or two, and your domain settings), and I'll be honest about each one.

1

Step 1: Decide the one job of your site

Most small business sites try to do everything and end up doing nothing. Pick one job. Mine is: turn a visitor into a contact and give them a free guide. Yours might be "get a quote request" or "book a call."

Copy this inHelp me decide the one job of my website before we build anything. My business: [what you do, in one or two sentences] Who visits: [who they are and what they want when they land] What I want them to do: [book a call, request a quote, join my list, buy, call me] What I can offer for free: [a checklist, a guide, a free estimate, a first consult, or nothing yet] Ask me questions one at a time until you understand my business. Then give me: 1. The one job of the site, in one sentence. 2. The single action every page should lead to. 3. What a visitor needs to believe before they take that action. 4. Anything on my current site (if I have one) that distracts from that job.

Write the answer down. Every later prompt points back to it.

A quick example. A two-person bookkeeping firm decides the job is "get small business owners to book a free 20-minute books review." Every page now ends with the same button. The services page stops listing every service and starts answering "is my mess too big for you?"

2

Step 2: Plan the pages

Copy this inUsing the one job we just agreed, plan my website. Keep it small: 3 to 6 pages. For each page give me: - The page name and its web address (like /services). - Its purpose in one sentence. - The sections, top to bottom, with what each section says in plain words. - The button or form on that page and where it goes. Then tell me which of these I need on day one and which can wait. If something repeats (like services, guides, team members or reviews), tell me, because I want those to come from one simple list I can add to later.

That last line matters. On my site the guides are one list. Each guide is one entry, and the library page and the guide pages are built from it. That's why adding a guide takes minutes.

3

Step 3: Write the copy in your voice

Give Claude real examples of how you talk. A few emails to customers or a transcript of you explaining your service is enough.

Copy this inWrite the words for every page in the plan. How I talk: [plain, warm, direct, funny... and paste 2 or 3 real messages you have written to customers] Things I can honestly claim: [years in business, real reviews, real results, certifications] Things I must not claim: [anything I cannot prove, guarantees I do not offer] Rules: - Short sentences. No buzzwords. Write like I talk. - Every page leads to the one action we agreed. - Only use claims from my list. If a section needs proof I haven't given you, write [ADD REAL EXAMPLE] instead of inventing it. - Give me a headline and two alternatives for each page.

Read it out loud. If a line sounds like a brochure, tell Claude "say that the way I'd say it to a customer on the phone."

4

Step 4: Have Claude build it

You have two ways to do this.

  • Easiest to start: ask Claude in the normal chat to build the site as HTML files. You copy each file into a folder on your computer. Fine for a small site.
  • Better if you're not technical: use Claude Code. It runs on your computer, creates the files and folders for you, and can open the site so you can look at it. You describe what you want in plain words. I build my sites this way.
Copy this inBuild my website from the page plan and the copy above. Requirements: - A simple static site: plain HTML, CSS and a little JavaScript. No paid builder and no database. - Looks good on a phone first. - Style: [colors, fonts, or a site you like the look of]. Clean, lots of white space, easy to read. - Anything that repeats (like [services, guides, reviews]) comes from one data file, so I can add an item by adding one entry, without touching the page code. - Put every page in one folder I can upload as is. - Leave the form ready to connect to my CRM. Mark clearly where the connection goes. When you're done, list every file, what it does, and how I add a new item to the data file. Explain it like I've never built a website.

Look at every page on your phone. Ask for changes in plain words: "make the button bigger," "move the reviews above the price."

5

Step 5: Connect your form to your CRM or email tool

A static site doesn't store sign-ups itself. The form has to send them somewhere. Two common ways:

  • A webhook. Your CRM gives you a web address that accepts new contacts. My forms post to a GoHighLevel inbound webhook, and a workflow there adds the contact, tags it and sends the guide email. HubSpot, Zapier, Make and others offer the same idea.
  • The tool's own form. Most email tools (Mailchimp, Kit, ActiveCampaign and others) give you a form you can paste into a page. Simpler, a little less control over the look.
Copy this inI use [your CRM or email tool]. Walk me through connecting my website form to it, click by click. Assume I'm not technical. The form collects: [first name, email, phone, what they need] When someone submits it, I want: [them added as a contact, tagged with where they came from, and sent a welcome or delivery email] Then update the form code to send to that connection, show a thank-you message, and tell me how to test it with my own email before I go live.

Submit the form yourself and check the contact shows up in your CRM. Never paste a secret key or password into the website code. A webhook address made for public forms is fine. Anything labeled "secret" or "private" is not.

6

Step 6: Publish it free and point your domain

Hosting a static site can be free. GitHub Pages, Netlify and Cloudflare Pages all have free tiers. Mine is on GitHub Pages. This is the part with real one-time setup: you'll create an account, upload your folder, and change a setting at the company where you bought your domain. Plan an hour the first time.

Copy this inI want to publish my website folder for free on [GitHub Pages, Netlify or Cloudflare Pages] and use my domain [yourdomain.com], which I bought at [GoDaddy, Namecheap, Squarespace, etc.]. Walk me through it one step at a time, click by click. Wait for me to say "done" before giving me the next step. Include: 1. Creating the account and uploading the site. 2. The exact domain (DNS) records to add at my domain company, and what each one does. 3. Turning on the secure lock (HTTPS). 4. How to check it's working, and how long changes can take to show up.

If you're using Claude Code, it can do most of the upload steps for you. You'll still log in to your accounts and change the domain settings yourself.

7

Step 7: Add pages without starting over (and the mistakes to avoid)

This is where the one data file pays off. A new service, guide or review is one new entry.

Copy this inHere's a new item for my site: [paste the details of the new service, guide or review]. Add it to the data file in the same format as the others. Write any copy it needs in my voice. Then tell me exactly what changed and how to publish the update.

For bigger changes, start with the job again: "I want to add a page for [topic]. Does it help the one job of my site? If yes, plan it the same way as the others."

Common mistakes:

  • Building before deciding the one job. You get a pretty site that doesn't bring in contacts.
  • Letting AI invent reviews, results or numbers. Only real claims.
  • Never testing the form. Send yourself a test every time you change it.
  • Putting private keys or passwords in the site files.
  • Too many pages on day one. Launch small, then add.
