The AI Operating System Blueprint: 5 steps to AI agents that run your business

Everybody wants AI agents running their business: generating leads, creating content, following up, closing sales, running operations. Then you try to build it and drown in agents, workflows, automations, integrations and APIs. You end up spending more time building AI than running the business you wanted automated.

An AI operating system fixes the order. You don't start with tools. You start with your business, and you build it like dominoes:

Lead comes in
→
AI qualifies
→
Follows up
→
Books the call
→
Updates your CRM
→
Triggers the next step

One action sets off the whole chain. AI runs each domino. You only step in for the important decisions.

Here are the 5 steps, in order, with the prompts to do each one. They work in Claude, ChatGPT or any AI. Use them in the same chat so it remembers what it learned.

1

Step 1: Ingest your business

AI can't run a business it doesn't understand. Before any agent exists, get what's in your head and your files into one place: your knowledge, strategy, SOPs, processes and goals.

Copy this inI'm building an AI operating system for my business. First you need to ingest my business. Interview me one question at a time about: 1) what I sell, the prices and who buys, 2) my strategy and goals for the next 12 months, 3) how a customer finds me, buys and gets delivered to, step by step, 4) the SOPs and processes my team already follows, 5) the tools I use, 6) what must never happen without my approval. Then write it up as my Business Brain: one document with a heading for each area.

Then feed it what you already have. Paste in or upload your existing SOPs, sales scripts, email templates, price sheet and onboarding docs, and say:

Copy this inHere are my existing documents. Add what's useful to my Business Brain, flag anything that contradicts what I told you, and list the gaps: processes my business clearly runs on that are not written down anywhere.

Save the result as business-brain. Every agent you build reads it first.

2

Step 2: Map your company

Now turn the business into a map: every department, and the outcome each one owns.

Copy this inUsing my Business Brain, map my company. Use these departments unless my business needs different ones: Marketing, Sales, Fulfillment, Operations. For each department give me: the outcomes it owns (results I can measure, like "every new lead gets a reply in 5 minutes"), the work that happens every day or week to hit them, and who or what does that work today. Put it in one table per department. Then rank the outcomes by how much time or money they cost me right now.

Save this as company-map. The top-ranked outcome is where your first domino chain goes.

3

Step 3: Design the AI workforce

For each department you design three things: an AI manager who owns the outcome, specialized agents that each do one job, and the workflow that moves work between them.

Copy this inDesign the AI workforce for my [department] department. Give me: 1) one AI manager: its job in one sentence, what it checks every day, what it reports to me, 2) the specialized agents under it, each with ONE job, what triggers it, what it needs, what it produces, and who it hands the work to next, 3) the workflow: the order the work moves in, from the first trigger to the finished outcome. Draw the workflow as a simple numbered chain.

Then make each agent reliable. An agent is only as good as its instructions:

Copy this inWrite the SOP for the [agent name] agent: the trigger, the inputs it reads, the exact steps, the output format with an example, the quality check it runs before handing off, and when it must stop and bring the decision to me.

Do one department all the way through before you start the next.

4

Step 4: Connect everything

This is the plumbing. Agents get useful when they can see your real leads, emails, calendar and records.

  1. List the pipes. For your first department, write down the tools the work touches: your CRM, email, calendar, database or spreadsheet, and anything else.
  2. Connect them where your AI lives. Claude and ChatGPT both have built-in connectors for tools like Gmail, Google Calendar and Google Drive (look under settings). In Claude Code, tools connect through MCP, an open standard many apps now support. Automation tools like Zapier and Make bridge to thousands more.
  3. One pipe at a time, read-only first. Let the agent read before you let it write.
Copy this inMy [department] agents need to work with these tools: [your CRM, email, calendar, database]. For each tool, tell me the simplest way to connect it to [Claude / ChatGPT / Claude Code], walk me through it step by step assuming I'm not technical, and tell me exactly what each agent should be allowed to read and what it should be allowed to change. Start read-only.

Nothing sends to a customer and nothing spends money without your OK until you trust it.

5

Step 5: Test, deploy and oversee

Don't flip the whole thing on. Test it, deploy it in draft mode, then let the workflows run while you oversee the decisions that matter.

Test itHere are 3 real examples from last month: [paste 3 real leads, emails or tasks]. Run each one through the workflow exactly as designed. Show me what each agent produced at each step. Then tell me where it broke, what was missing from the SOPs, and fix the SOPs.
Deploy itWrite my decision rules for this department in three lists: 1) what the agents do on their own, 2) what they draft and wait for my approval on, 3) what always comes straight to me. Then write the daily brief you'll give me every morning: what got done, what's waiting on me, and the decisions only I can make. Keep it under 10 lines.

Run it in draft mode for two weeks. Every time it gets something wrong, fix the SOP, not just the answer. Then move items from "wait for my approval" to "do on their own" one at a time.

6

Build your first domino chain this week

You don't need the whole operating system to feel this work. Build one chain, end to end, on your top-ranked outcome. New leads is the best place to start for most businesses.

Copy this inBuild my first domino chain for this outcome: [your top outcome, for example: every new lead gets followed up until they book]. Design it as dominoes: the trigger that starts it, then each step as its own agent with one job, what it hands to the next, what gets logged in my CRM, and the exact point where I approve before anything goes to a customer. Then give me the setup checklist to have this running in 7 days, starting with what I do today.

One chain running is worth more than ten planned. Once it holds, add the next domino chain, then the next department. That is how the operating system gets built.
