The Pain-to-Content Matrix: map every customer problem to a video brief with Claude

Stop guessing what video to make next. Map every problem your customer has, match each one to what your business actually does about it, and have Claude turn every match into a video brief someone can pick up and film.

I did this for my job-search company. My business helps people get hired, so I asked Claude to list every stage of a job search, from picking a career to negotiating the offer. That came to 11 stages. Then, for every stage, the real pain in my customer's words, like "I've sent 40 applications and heard nothing" or "I freeze in interviews." Then I matched each pain to what my product does about it, and Claude wrote a full brief for each one: hook, script, what to film and the call to action. Twenty-six briefs. My creators pick one and film it, and I don't start from a blank page anymore.

Every video follows the same order: problem first, then the solution, then the result. Claude is the main tool here, and every prompt works the same in ChatGPT. Use one chat for the whole thing so it keeps the context.

1

Step 1: Tell Claude about your business

Claude does far better work when it knows who you serve. Start a new chat and paste this in.

Copy this inI run a business called [business name]. We help [who your customer is] get [the result they want]. What we sell: [your product or service, in one or two sentences] Our customer is usually: [their role, their situation, how they feel when they find us] Things I can honestly claim: [real results, real experience, real facts only] Things I must not claim: [anything you can't prove, income promises, guarantees you don't offer] Don't write anything yet. Reply with a short summary of how you understand my business and my customer, then ask me up to five questions that would help you do better work.

Answer the questions. It takes two minutes and makes every later step better.

2

Step 2: Map every stage of the customer's journey

Now map the whole path your customer walks, from the first moment they feel the problem to the moment they get the result. Mine had 11 stages. Yours might have 8 or 15.

Copy this inList every stage a customer goes through on the way to [the result they want], from the first moment they realize they have a problem to the moment they get the result, and what comes right after. Rules: - Put the stages in order. - Use plain words, not industry terms. - Each stage is one clear moment or decision, not a broad phase like "awareness." - Aim for 8 to 15 stages. Give me a numbered list. Name each stage in five words or fewer, and add one sentence on what the customer is doing at that stage.

Read the list. Delete stages that don't fit, and add any Claude missed. You know your customer better than the AI does.

3

Step 3: Write the pain at each stage, in their words

This is the most important step. A pain written in marketing language makes a weak video. A pain written the way a real person says it out loud makes a strong hook.

Copy this inFor each stage in the list above, write the real pains my customer feels there. Rules: - Write each pain as something the customer would actually say out loud, in first person, in quotes. - Everyday spoken language. No jargon. - Be specific. "I'm stuck" is too vague. "I've sent 40 applications and heard nothing" is the right level. - Give two or three pains per stage where there are clear ones. Format as a table with three columns: Stage number, Stage name, Pain in the customer's words.

Now check the pains against reality. Pull up customer emails and messages, questions people ask on calls, comments on your posts, and reviews of you and your competitors. Swap in the exact phrases real customers use.

Copy this inHere are real things my customers have said, copied from emails, calls, comments and reviews: [paste 10 to 30 real quotes, with names removed] Update the pain table. Replace any pain that a real quote says better, add new pains these quotes reveal, and mark each row "real quote" or "AI guess" so I know which ones to double-check.
4

Step 4: Match each pain to what you really do

A video only works if it solves something. Here you connect each pain to something your business actually does.

Copy this inHere's what my product or service actually does: [list the real features, services or parts of your process. Only list what really exists.] For each pain in the table, match it to the one thing from my list that helps most. Rules: - Only use things from my list. Don't invent features. - If a pain has no match, write "No match." - Add one short sentence on how it helps. Add two columns to the table: What we do about it, and How it helps.

Look closely at the "No match" rows. Each one is either a pain you can still help with through free advice in a video, or a gap in what you offer. Both are worth knowing.

5

Step 5: Turn every row into a video brief

Now Claude turns each row into something a creator, a team member or you can film. Do one row first to check the quality.

Copy this inWrite a video brief for row [number] of the table. The video must follow this order: the problem first, then the solution, then the result. Include: 1. Hook: the first line, spoken in the first 6 seconds. It names the pain in the customer's own words. 2. Script: 35 to 60 seconds of spoken text. Short sentences. Plain words. No hype. 3. What to film: the shots or screen recordings needed, in order. 4. On-screen text: three to five short captions. 5. Call to action: one clear next step for the viewer. Rules: - Don't invent numbers, results or customer stories. If a line needs a real example, write [ADD YOUR REAL EXAMPLE]. - Don't promise outcomes I can't back up. - Write the way a person talks, not the way an ad reads.

If the first brief is good, run the rest in batches.

Copy this inNow do the same for rows [2 to 6]. Same format, same rules.

Work in batches of five or six. Very long answers tend to get weaker toward the end.

6

Step 6: Put it all in one sheet and hand it off

Copy this inCombine everything into one table I can paste into a spreadsheet. Columns: Stage, Pain, What we do about it, Hook, Script, What to film, On-screen text, Call to action. Use tab-separated format so it pastes cleanly into Google Sheets or Excel.

Paste it into a sheet. That's your matrix. Add two columns for yourself: Status (to film, filmed, posted) and Date posted. Give the sheet to whoever films. They pick a row, film it and mark it done.

Want it as a page instead of a sheet? In Claude you can ask for it as an interactive page.

Copy this inTurn this matrix into a simple interactive page. Show the stages as a row of tabs along the top. Clicking a stage shows its pains. Clicking a pain opens its full brief. Keep the design plain and easy to read on a phone.

That's how mine works: a stage rail across the top, the pain column, and one brief opened up when you click it.

7

Step 7: A worked example, and the mistakes to skip

A quick example. Say you run a home-cleaning company for busy families, offering weekly and biweekly visits with the same cleaner each time.

  • A few stages: realizing the house is out of control, deciding to get help, comparing cleaners, worrying about strangers in the home, booking the first visit, getting ready for the first visit.
  • Pains: "I spend my whole Saturday cleaning and it's a mess again by Tuesday." "I don't want someone I don't know walking around my house." "I have to clean before the cleaner comes? That defeats the point."
  • Matches: weekly visits that give the weekend back. The same cleaner every visit. A short list of what to leave as is before the first visit (only if that's really how the business works).
  • One brief: Hook: "Do you clean your house before the cleaner comes?" Script: the problem, what you actually need to do before a first visit, the result. Film: the cleaner arriving, a lived-in room, the finished room. Call to action: "Message us to book your first visit."

One row became one video. Every other row becomes another.

Common mistakes:

  • Skipping Step 1. The pains come out generic.
  • Keeping AI-guessed pains without checking them against real customer words.
  • Letting AI invent results, numbers or testimonials. Delete anything you can't prove.
  • Matching pains to things you don't offer.
  • Starting the video with your product. Problem first, always.
  • Building the matrix and never filming. Film one row this week.

Come back every few months, run Step 3 again with fresh customer quotes, and add new rows.
