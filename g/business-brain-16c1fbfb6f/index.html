The Business Brain: have Claude interview you and write the one document that teaches AI your business

If you use AI and keep getting generic answers, the AI isn't the problem. It doesn't know your business yet. You wouldn't hire someone and give them zero training, but that's what most people do with Claude.

So give it a brain. One document that holds what you sell, your prices, who you sell to, how you talk, real examples of your writing, and what you never say. You don't write it yourself. Claude interviews you one question at a time and writes it from your answers. Then it goes into a Claude Project, and every chat in that Project already knows your business. Same AI. Completely different answers.

Everything here works the same in ChatGPT with a Project or a custom GPT.

1

Step 1: Let Claude interview you

Set aside about half an hour with no interruptions. Open a new chat and paste this in.

Copy this inI want you to build a "business brain" for my business: one document any AI chat can read to understand my business and write like me. Interview me. Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer before the next. Start broad, then go deeper. If an answer is vague, ask me for a real example. Cover these areas, in this order: 1. The business: what we do, in one sentence, and how long we've done it. 2. What we sell: every product or service, what's included, and the price. 3. Customers: who buys, the problem they have before they find us, how they describe it in their own words, and why they choose us. 4. Who we don't serve: customers or jobs we turn down. 5. How people buy: how they find us, the steps to buy, and the common questions or objections. 6. Proof: real results, reviews or facts we can honestly use. 7. How we talk: our tone, words we use, words we avoid. 8. What we never say: claims, promises, topics or words that are off limits. 9. Team and tools: who does what, and the tools we use. 10. Anything else a new hire would need to know in their first week. Tell me which area we're in as we go. When we've covered everything, say "Ready to write" and wait for me.

Talk, don't polish. Typos are fine. Use voice dictation if it's faster.

2

Step 2: Give it real examples of your writing

Rules about your tone help a little. Real examples help a lot. Gather three to five things you wrote yourself: a customer email, a social post, a text to a client, a page from your website.

Copy this inHere are [number] real examples of my writing. Study them and describe my voice as plain rules: sentence length, words and phrases I use often, how I open and close, how formal I am, and what I never do. Then quote one short line from each example that sounds most like me. These go in the business brain. [paste your examples, each with a one-line note on what it is]
3

Step 3: Have Claude write the document

Copy this in when the interview is doneWrite my business brain now, using only what I told you in this chat. Use this exact template: [Business name] business brain Last updated: [date] 1. The business in one paragraph 2. What we sell (each offer: name, what's included, price, who it's for) 3. Our customers (who they are, their problem in their own words, why they choose us) 4. Who we don't serve 5. How people buy (how they find us, the steps, top questions and our answers) 6. Proof we can use (only real results, reviews and facts) 7. How we talk (voice rules) 8. Examples of our writing (the lines from my real writing) 9. What we never say 10. Team and tools 11. Open questions (anything you weren't sure about) Rules: plain language, short bullet points, nothing invented. If you don't have an answer for a section, write MISSING there instead of guessing.
4

Step 4: Check it and fill the gaps

Copy this inReview the business brain you just wrote. List every section marked MISSING, anything that sounds vague or generic, anything that contradicts something else in the document, and any price or claim I should double-check. Ask me about each one, one at a time, then give me the updated document.

Then read the final version yourself. If a line isn't true, fix it now. Save it as a document called business-brain.

5

Step 5: Put it in a Claude Project

  1. Create a new Project in Claude. Name it after your business.
  2. Upload the business-brain document to the Project knowledge.
  3. Paste the instructions below into the Project instructions.
  4. In ChatGPT, do the same with a Project or a custom GPT.
Copy this into the Project instructionsYou work for [business name]. Before every answer, read the business-brain document in this Project. Use our real offers, prices, customers and voice from it, and write the way the examples in it sound. Never say anything from the "what we never say" section. Never invent results, reviews, prices or facts. If an answer needs something that isn't in the business brain, tell me what's missing and ask me instead of guessing.

Now every chat you start inside that Project already knows your business. You never paste your background in again.

6

Step 6: See the difference (before and after)

Here's what that looks like for a small bookkeeping firm that works with local restaurants.

Before, in a normal chat: "Write a follow-up email to a lead who asked about our services." Claude writes a polite, generic email about "streamlining your finances" and "tailored solutions". It could be from any firm anywhere.

After, in the Project with the business brain, same request. Claude writes a short email in the owner's voice. It names the monthly bookkeeping package and its real price, speaks to the restaurant owner's actual problem (doing the books late at night after closing), answers the question most leads ask about switching from their current bookkeeper, and skips the words the owner never uses. Same AI. The only thing that changed is what it knows.

Try this in a normal chat, then in your ProjectWrite a follow-up email to a lead who asked about [your main offer] but hasn't booked yet.

Compare the two answers side by side. That gap is what the business brain gives you.

7

Step 7: Keep it updated, and the rules

A business brain goes stale when your prices, offers or customers change. Update it the day something changes, and do a quick check once a month.

Copy this in when something changesHere's my current business brain: [paste it]. Here's what's changed: [new prices, a new offer, a new type of customer, a phrase I've stopped using]. Update only the sections that need it, change the "Last updated" date, and show me exactly what you changed.

Then replace the old file in the Project knowledge with the new one.

Copy this in once a monthHere are answers from the past month that I had to fix, and what I changed: [paste a few]. What do my fixes have in common? Suggest additions to the business brain so I don't have to make those fixes again.

Rules and common mistakes:

  • One brain, one document. Don't scatter it across ten files.
  • Real examples of your writing beat descriptions of your voice.
  • Never let it make up proof. Use MISSING and fill it in yourself.
  • Keep private data out: no passwords, bank details or private customer information.
  • Update prices the day they change.
  • Reuse it everywhere. Every Project and agent you build can read the same business brain.
