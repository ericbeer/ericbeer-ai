The AI Avatar Playbook: put an AI host on camera for your business, the honest way

Tired of filming every video yourself? An AI host can be on camera for you. You give an AI video tool one photo, one voice and a script, and it makes a talking video.

I made one. Albeal is the AI host for Career Report. Albeal has one photo and one voice, kept the same in every video. I used Higgsfield, but plenty of AI video tools do this, and you don't need any one in particular. What matters is the workflow: Claude writes the host's profile and every script, every clip gets checked word by word so the lips match, and every video is labeled as AI.

This guide gives you all of it, including what my test cost and the rules that keep your audience's trust. Claude is the main tool, and every prompt works the same in ChatGPT.

1

Step 1: Decide who your host is, and what it never says

A host with a clear job is easier to script and easier to trust. Write the profile first.

Copy this inI run a [type of business] that serves [type of customer]. I want an AI host for my short videos. The host will always be clearly labeled as AI. Write a one-page host profile. Include: 1. Five name options for the host. 2. The host's job in one sentence (for example: "explains one tip per video"). 3. How the host speaks: sentence length, tone, words to use, words to avoid. 4. Five topics the host will cover. 5. Things the host will never say or claim. Always include these: - Never claims personal results, personal experience or a personal story ("I tried this and it worked for me"). - Never gives a testimonial or speaks as a customer. - Never claims to be a real person, a real employee or a real expert. - Never promises results or makes income claims. 6. One short AI label line the host says or shows in every video. Plain language. No hype words.

Pick a name and save the profile in a note. You'll paste it into Claude every time you write a script.

Why the "never" list matters: an AI host hasn't done anything. It didn't use your service, didn't get results and isn't a customer. If a script has the host say "this changed my life," that's a fake testimonial, even if a real customer said something like it. Real results come from real people, on camera as themselves, with their permission.

2

Step 2: Lock one photo and one voice

This is what makes the host feel like the same person every time.

  • Choose one reference photo: well lit, facing the camera, plain background. Save it in a folder called "Host." Use that exact file every time.
  • Choose one voice. Write down the exact voice name and settings.
  • Never swap either one between videos. Small changes make the host feel like a stranger.
  • Only use a face and a voice you have the right to use: an AI-generated face, your own face or voice, or one your tool gives you the rights to use. Never copy a real person who hasn't agreed to it.
Copy this inUsing the host profile below, write a one-page consistency sheet I can keep open while I make videos. Include: - The photo file name to use: [file name] - The voice name and settings: [voice name and settings] - Speaking pace, roughly in words per minute - Video length range - The AI label wording, and where it goes on screen and in the caption - A checklist of six things to confirm before I generate any clip Host profile: [paste your host profile]
3

Step 3: Write the script with Claude

Keep scripts short. Short sentences help the lips match the words, because the tool has less to get wrong.

Copy this inWrite a script for my AI host. Use the host profile below. Topic: [one topic, one idea] Length: 35 to 60 seconds spoken Rules: - Plain, direct spoken English. Short sentences. - No hype words. No em dashes. - First line names a problem my customer has. - Give one clear tip they can use today. - End with one call to action: [what you want viewers to do] - Include the AI label line at the start. - Follow the host's "never" list. No personal results, no personal stories, no testimonials. If a point needs proof, write [REAL PROOF FROM THE BUSINESS] and I'll add a true fact or cut it. Host profile: [paste your host profile] Write it as plain spoken lines, one sentence per line, with no stage directions. Then list any words a voice tool might mispronounce.

Read it out loud. If you stumble on a sentence, the voice tool probably will too. Ask Claude to simplify that line.

4

Step 4: Fix hard words, then make one test clip

Copy this inHere's my script. Rewrite any word a voice tool might mispronounce (names, brand terms, acronyms) so it sounds right when spoken. Use phonetic spelling only where needed. Keep the meaning the same. Show me a before-and-after list of every change. Script: [paste script]

Now upload your photo, pick your saved voice, paste the script and generate one clip. Don't batch ten yet. One test tells you whether the photo, voice and script work together before you spend more.

Budget honestly. Most AI video tools charge in credits. My test with Albeal used about 150 video credits. Your number will be different: credits vary by tool, by plan and by clip length, and failed clips you regenerate cost credits too. Check the credit cost per clip before you start, and write down what each clip actually used.

5

Step 5: Check the lips word by word

This is the step people skip, and viewers notice fast. Every clip gets checked word by word before it's posted. Play it slowly with headphones on and compare what you hear to the script. Look for:

  • Lips closing on "b," "p" and "m" sounds.
  • A mouth that stays open after the voice stops.
  • Words where the mouth moves with no sound, or the other way round.
  • Odd blinks, head jumps or a face that shifts between frames.
Copy this inMake a word-by-word lip-sync review sheet for this script. Break it into lines of 5 to 8 words. For each line give me: a checkbox for "lips match," a space for the timestamp if something is off, and a space for a note. At the end, add a pass or fail rule, for example how many flagged lines mean I regenerate the clip. Script: [paste script]

If a line fails, a shorter sentence or different punctuation often fixes it.

Copy this inThis line didn't sync well in my video tool: "[paste the line]" Give me three versions with the same meaning. Make each one shorter, with simpler words and nothing hard to pronounce.
6

Step 6: Label it AI, every time

No exceptions. Labeling protects your audience's trust, and Instagram and TikTok expect it.

  • On the post screen: Instagram and TikTok both have an AI label setting when you post. Turn it on for every AI host video. The menus move around, so if you don't see it, look under the advanced or "more options" settings before you post.
  • On screen: a short text label such as "AI host" for the whole video.
  • In the video or the caption: one plain line, such as "I'm the AI host for [your business]."
  • Never present the host as a real employee, a real customer or a real person.
Copy this inWrite five short, honest ways to label my AI host. Each under 10 words. Include options for on-screen text, a spoken line and a caption line. My host's name is [host name] and my business is [business name].
7

Step 7: Post, review, and the mistakes to skip

A few days after posting, paste what you can see into Claude.

Copy this inHere's how my AI host video did. I'm sharing only what I can see, not guesses. Topic: [topic] Where I posted: [Instagram, TikTok or other] What I can see: [views, comments, questions people asked] Tell me what to keep, what to change and what topic to try next. Don't make up numbers or assume results I didn't give you.

A quick example. A home-cleaning company creates "Mia," whose job is one cleaning tip per video. Her "never" list: no "I clean my own house this way," no customer stories, no promises. Her script on shower glass opens with "Hi, I'm Mia, the AI host for Bright Home Cleaning," gives one tip (wipe the glass after every shower), and ends with "Follow for more tips." In the lip check, "squeegee" looks off, so Claude swaps it for "a glass wiper" and that one clip is regenerated. "AI host" stays on screen the whole time, and the AI label is switched on when she posts. When the company wants proof, it films a real customer, as themselves, with permission.

Common mistakes:

  • Changing the photo or voice between videos.
  • Long, complicated sentences. More lip-sync problems.
  • Skipping the word-by-word check.
  • Generating lots of clips before testing one.
  • Letting the host claim results, experience or testimonials.
  • Forgetting the AI label on the post screen.
  • Using a face or voice you don't have the rights to.

Start small: one host, one photo, one voice, one clip. Get that working before anything else.
