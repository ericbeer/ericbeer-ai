The SOP-to-Agent Kit: turn one written process into an AI agent that does the job your way

Most owners are the bottleneck. Every task runs through them because nobody else does it the right way. The fix isn't working harder. It's writing down how you do one job and turning it into an AI agent that does it your way.

Here's the method. Take one written process. Have Claude turn it into instructions for an agent. Put those instructions in a Claude Project. Test it, and when it gets something wrong, fix the process, not the answer. One SOP, one agent. Then repeat. No code. Every step works the same in ChatGPT with a Project or a custom GPT.

An SOP (standard operating procedure) is just a written process: the steps someone follows to do a job. If yours only lives in your head, Step 2 has Claude interview you and write it.

1

Step 1: Pick one process to start with

Pick a job that repeats every week, follows roughly the same steps each time, and that you're tired of doing or checking yourself. Good first picks: replying to new quote requests, writing a weekly client update, turning call notes into a follow-up email, or checking an order before it ships.

Skip anything that moves money, needs legal judgment, or needs access you aren't ready to give. Start small.

Copy this inHelp me pick the first process to turn into an AI agent. Here are the jobs I do every week: [list them, even roughly]. For each one, score it from 1 to 5 on: how often it happens, how much of my time it takes, how much it follows the same steps each time, and how low the risk is if a draft is wrong. Show a table, then recommend one to start with and tell me why.
2

Step 2: No SOP yet? Let Claude interview you

Copy this inI want to write an SOP (standard operating procedure) for this job: [job name]. I've never written it down. Interview me to get it out of my head. Ask me one question at a time. Wait for my answer before asking the next one. Cover: - What starts this job (the trigger) - What information or files I need before I start - Every step I take, in order - The decisions I make along the way and how I make them - What a great result looks like, and what a bad one looks like - Mistakes new people make - When I'd stop and ask someone, or say no - Who gets the finished work, and in what format If an answer is vague, ask me for a real example. When you have enough, write the SOP in plain numbered steps, and list anything you're still unsure about.

Answer the way you'd explain it to a new hire. Use voice dictation if typing is slow. Real examples are worth more than rules.

3

Step 3: Turn the SOP into agent instructions

This is the main prompt. Paste your SOP in at the bottom.

Copy this inTurn this SOP into instructions for an AI agent that does this job for my business. Write the instructions to the agent, starting with "You are...". Include these sections: 1. The job: one or two sentences on what this agent does and for whom. 2. What you'll be given: the inputs it should expect each time. 3. The steps: numbered, in order, taken from the SOP. 4. What good looks like: a clear description of a great result, plus a short checklist the agent runs before it answers. 5. When to stop and ask me: the exact situations where it must not guess and must ask instead. 6. Never do: things it must never do or say. 7. The output: the exact format of what it hands back. Keep every detail from the SOP. Don't add steps I didn't describe. If something in the SOP is unclear or missing, list it as a question at the end instead of filling the gap yourself. My SOP: [paste your SOP]

Answer the questions at the end, then ask Claude to update the instructions with your answers. Save the final version.

4

Step 4: Put it in a Claude Project

  1. In Claude, create a new Project and name it after the job, like "Quote Reply Agent".
  2. Paste the agent instructions into the Project instructions.
  3. Add knowledge files: the SOP itself, your price or service list if the job needs it, two or three examples of great past work, and any templates.
  4. In ChatGPT: create a Project or a custom GPT, paste the same instructions, and upload the same files.

Now that one job has its own agent. Open a chat in the Project, give it the inputs, and it works from your instructions every time. On a plan that lets you share Projects, or by sharing a custom GPT, anyone on your team can use it too.

5

Step 5: Test it on real past cases

Don't trust it yet. Give it three to five real jobs from the past where you know what the right result looked like. Remove names and private details first.

Copy this in[paste a real past request] Do this job using your instructions. After your answer, show me the checklist from "what good looks like" with each item marked done or not done, and tell me anything you weren't sure about.

Compare each answer to what you, or your best person, would have done. Write down every gap.

Example. A small cleaning company turns its "reply to a new quote request" SOP into an agent. The Project holds the SOP, the price ranges by home size, and three of the owner's best past replies. First test: a request for a deep clean of a 3-bedroom home with a dog. The agent writes a friendly reply with the right price range and asks for the move-in date. But it offers a Saturday slot, and the owner always checks Saturdays with the team first. The SOP never said so. That's a gap in the SOP, not a bad answer.

6

Step 6: Fix the SOP, not the answer

When the agent gets something wrong, the easy move is to fix the answer and move on. Don't. Fix the instructions, so it never makes that mistake again.

Copy this inHere's what you did on this test: [paste the agent's answer] Here's what it should have done: [what you'd have done, and why] Don't redo the answer. Tell me which part of the instructions or the SOP caused the gap, and write the exact change to fix it: a new step, a clearer rule, or a new "stop and ask me" case. Show me only the updated section.

Paste the change into the Project instructions and the SOP file, then run the same test again. In the cleaning example, the fix is one new "stop and ask me" line: weekend slots need the owner's OK. When it passes all your test cases, it's ready to use. Keep a person checking anything that goes to a customer until it's earned your trust.

7

Step 7: Repeat, then follow the rules

One SOP, one agent. Do that ten times and you've got a team. Each new agent goes faster, because now you know the steps.

Copy this inHere are the agents I've built so far: [list]. Here are the other jobs in my business: [list]. Which three should I turn into agents next, and in what order? For each, tell me what SOP I need, what files the Project needs, and how it hands work to or from the agents I already have.

Rules and common mistakes:

  • One agent, one job. An agent that does five jobs does all of them badly.
  • Always include "when to stop and ask me". That's what keeps it safe.
  • Test on real past cases before any customer sees its work.
  • Fix the SOP, not the answer.
  • Keep a person reviewing anything that goes to a customer until you trust it.
  • Never paste passwords, card numbers or private customer data into a chat.
  • Update the Project the day your process changes.
