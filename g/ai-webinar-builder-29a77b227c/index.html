The AI Webinar Builder: plan your whole webinar with Claude, from the promise to the follow-up emails

A webinar has a lot of moving parts: the offer, the title, the script, the slides, the registration page, the reminder emails and the follow-up. Most owners either build all of it by hand over weeks or never run one at all.

I planned a full webinar launch for one of my businesses with AI: the script, the words on every slide, the run of show, the registration page, the reminder emails and the follow-up emails. The trick is to build it one piece at a time, in the same chat, so every piece knows about the pieces before it.

Below are the prompts in order. Use Claude (a Claude Project is perfect for this, so everything stays in one place). ChatGPT works the same. You still decide the offer and you still show up and teach. AI just takes the blank pages away.

1

Step 1: Pick the offer and the one promise

Every good webinar makes one promise and leads to one next step. Get these right first and everything else gets easier.

Copy this inI want to run a webinar for my business. Help me get the offer and the promise right before we write anything. My business: [what you do and who for] What I want people to do after the webinar: [book a call, buy a program, start a trial, join a waitlist] The price and what they get: [the real offer] Who should attend: [the exact type of person, and the problem they have right now] Real proof I can share: [real results, real client stories, your experience. Only true things.] Ask me questions one at a time until you understand it. Then give me: 1. The one promise of the webinar: what attendees will be able to do or understand by the end, in one sentence. 2. The three biggest beliefs that stop this person from buying, and how the webinar can honestly address each. 3. Why someone should attend live instead of skipping it. Don't promise results I can't back up.

Save the answer in the chat. Every later step uses it.

2

Step 2: Get title and hook options

Copy this inUsing the promise and the audience above, write: 1. Ten webinar titles. Mix styles: a how-to, a mistake to avoid, a number-of-steps title, a question. Each one under 12 words. 2. For my top three picks (you choose and say why), a one-line subtitle. 3. Five opening lines I could say in the first 30 seconds to make people stay. Plain words. No hype words like "secret," "explode" or "guaranteed." Nothing that implies a result I can't prove.

Read them out loud and pick the one you'd actually say to a customer.

3

Step 3: Build the outline and run of show

The run of show is the minute-by-minute plan. It keeps you on time and makes sure you get to the offer before people leave.

Copy this inWrite the outline and run of show for a [45 or 60] minute webinar with this title: [your title]. Give me a table with: start time, length, section name, what I say or do, and the goal of that section. Include: - A welcome that tells people what they'll get and to stay to the end for [the bonus, Q&A or offer]. - My short story and why they should listen to me (real facts only). - The teaching: 3 main points that deliver the promise. Each one gives real value on its own. - A natural bridge from the teaching to the offer. - The offer: what it is, who it's for, who it's not for, the price, and how to get it. - Live Q&A. - A clear close with the one next step. Also mark where I check the chat, where I share my screen, and where a co-host could help.
4

Step 4: Write the slide-by-slide words

Copy this inNow turn the run of show into slides. For each slide give me: - Slide number and section. - The words on the slide: a short headline and no more than 3 short lines. Big and simple. - What I say while it's up, in my voice, as speaker notes. - A note on any image, screenshot or example to show. Rules: - One idea per slide. - My voice: [how you talk, plus a sentence or two you have actually said] - If a slide needs proof or a story I haven't given you, write [ADD REAL EXAMPLE]. Don't make one up.

Paste the slide words into Google Slides, Keynote, PowerPoint or Canva. Ask Claude to "make slides 10 to 15 shorter" if anything feels crowded.

5

Step 5: Write the registration page

Copy this inWrite the registration page for this webinar. Date and time: [date, time and time zone] Where: [Zoom, a webinar tool, YouTube Live] Include, top to bottom: 1. A headline and subheadline from the title and promise. 2. Three to five bullets: what they'll learn. 3. Who it's for, and who it's not for. 4. A short bio of me with real facts only. 5. The sign-up form: ask for first name and email only. 6. A short FAQ: is it free, will there be a replay, how long is it, is there something to buy. 7. The thank-you page they see after signing up: what happens next and to add it to their calendar. Be honest that there will be an offer at the end. Keep it short.

Put this into whatever you already use: your website builder, your email tool's landing page, or your CRM.

A quick example. An interior design studio wants more full-room projects. The promise: "Plan a room that works before you buy a single piece." The three teaching points become the slides. The offer at the end is a paid design consult. The registration page is honest that the consult is offered at the end, and the reminder emails each share one small layout tip so people want to show up.

6

Step 6: Write the reminder emails (before)

Copy this inWrite the reminder emails for people who signed up. 1. Confirmation, sent right away: the date, time, link, add-to-calendar, and one reason to show up live. 2. One day before: what they'll learn, plus one small useful tip on the topic. 3. One hour before: short, the link, "see you soon." 4. Starting now: one line and the link. Optional: a text message version of the 1-hour and starting-now reminders, under 160 characters each. For each email give me three subject line options and the body. My voice, short, one clear link per email. No fake urgency.
7

Step 7: Write the replay and follow-up emails (after)

Copy this inWrite the follow-up emails after the webinar. Two groups: people who attended, and people who signed up but didn't attend. For both groups: 1. Same day: thank you, the replay link, and the offer link with a short recap. 2. Next day: answer the top questions from the Q&A: [paste the real questions people asked] 3. Day 3: one real story or example from my business that fits the offer: [a true story] 4. Final day: a clear, honest reminder of when the offer or replay closes: [the real deadline, or write none] Change the wording for people who didn't attend so it doesn't assume they saw it. One clear link per email. Never invent results, numbers or deadlines.

If there is no real deadline, don't make one up. People can tell.

8

Step 8: Practice and prep for the Q&A

Copy this inPretend you are a skeptical person in my audience: [describe them]. 1. Give me the 15 hardest questions or objections someone could ask during my webinar, from the most likely to the least. 2. For each, write a short, honest answer in my voice, based only on what I've told you. If you don't know the real answer, say "I need your answer here" instead of guessing. 3. Then read my run of show and slide words and tell me where people are most likely to get bored or leave, and how to fix it.

Then practice once out loud, with a timer. Fix anything that runs long.

Mistakes to avoid:

  • Building the slides before the promise. Step 1 decides everything.
  • All teaching and no offer, or an offer that comes out of nowhere. The bridge matters.
  • Invented proof, numbers or deadlines. Use only what's true.
  • Skipping the reminders. Many people sign up and forget.
  • Sending no-shows the same email as attendees.
