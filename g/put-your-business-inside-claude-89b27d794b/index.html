Put Your Business Inside Claude: connect your tools and data so Claude can work in your business

Your customers already ask AI for help. So I put my business where they already are: inside Claude.

My company Scout Jobs runs inside Claude as a connector. People add it in about a minute: in Claude they go to Customize, then Connectors, then Add custom connector, paste the Scout URL and sign in. From then on, Claude can use Scout's tools right in the chat. Behind that one chat are Scout, 35 AI managers and 154 specialists that run a job search.

You don't need anything that big to start. This guide takes you up three levels: turn on connectors for the tools you already use, connect more of your tools through an automation service, and then, when you're ready, have Claude Code help you build a connector for your own business. Every step has the exact prompt. Each level works on its own, so stop wherever you like.

1

Step 1: Understand what a connector is (in plain words)

A connector lets Claude use your business's data and actions. Without one, Claude only knows what you paste into the chat. With one, Claude can look things up and do things in your tools, with your permission.

  • Data means Claude can read: your calendar, your inbox, your files, your customer list.
  • Actions means Claude can do: draft an email, create a calendar event, add a contact, update a deal.

Under the hood, connectors use a standard called MCP (Model Context Protocol). You don't need to understand it. Just know that when a service says it "offers an MCP connection" or "an MCP server," it means Claude can plug into it.

Plan rules, honestly: custom connectors (the kind where you paste a URL, like Scout) are available on paid Claude plans, and the Free plan has been more limited. Team and Enterprise accounts need an Owner to add them. These rules change, so check Claude's help center for the current version before you start. ChatGPT has its own connector rules and its own menus.

2

Step 2: List what you'd want Claude to do

Before you connect anything, decide what's worth connecting. Start a new chat in Claude and paste this.

Copy this inI run a [type of business]. Here are the tools my business uses every day: [list them, for example: Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, QuickBooks, HubSpot, Jobber, Square, Notion] Here are the tasks I still do by hand that touch those tools: [for example: answering quote requests, booking jobs, sending invoices, checking who hasn't paid, writing weekly updates] Make a table with these columns: Task, Tool it needs, What Claude would read, What Claude would do, How much time it might save me (low, medium or high, no made-up numbers), and Risk if it goes wrong (low, medium or high). Then sort the table so the safest, most useful tasks are at the top. Mark which tools are common enough that they probably already have a ready-made connector. I'll check each one myself.

The top of that list is where you start. Pick tasks where Claude mostly reads and drafts, not ones where it sends money or deletes things.

3

Step 3: Level 1, turn on connectors for tools you already use

Claude has a directory of ready-made connectors for popular tools like Gmail, Google Drive, Google Calendar, Notion and HubSpot, plus many more. This is the easiest win.

  1. Open Claude on your computer (the website or the desktop app).
  2. Click Customize, then Connectors. (In some versions it's under Settings, then Connectors.)
  3. Browse the directory and find a tool from your Step 2 list.
  4. Click Connect, sign in to that tool on its own page, and approve what it can access. Your password goes into the tool's page, never into the chat.
  5. In a chat, click the + button, open Connectors, and make sure the one you added is switched on.

Start with one or two. You can always add more.

4

Step 4: Put your connectors to work

Now try real work. Start with a read-only task, so you can see what Claude finds before you let it act.

Copy this inLook through my email from the last 7 days and find every message from a customer or a lead that hasn't had a reply from me yet. List them with the sender, the date, what they asked for and how urgent it seems. Don't send anything.
Copy this inFor the first three emails on that list, draft a reply in my voice. Keep each under 80 words, answer what they asked and end with one clear next step. Leave them as drafts. Don't send. My voice: [friendly and short / direct and professional]. Facts you can use: [hours, prices, service area, booking link]. If you need a fact I haven't given you, write [CHECK] instead of guessing.
Copy this inCheck my calendar for next week. Find three open 30-minute slots between [start time] and [end time] for a call with a new client, and write a short message offering those times.

About permissions: Claude asks before it uses a connector's tools, and you can decide per tool whether it should always ask, always be allowed, or never be used. Keep "ask" on for anything that sends, publishes, pays or deletes. Only switch read-only tools to always allow, once you trust them.

5

Step 5: Level 2, connect more tools through Zapier or Make

Your scheduling software, invoicing tool or industry app might not be in Claude's directory. Automation services like Zapier and Make already connect to thousands of apps, and both offer an MCP connection, which means Claude can use them as a bridge.

  1. In Zapier or Make, find their MCP option (the names and menus change, so search their help pages for "MCP").
  2. Choose exactly which actions Claude is allowed to use. For example: "find a customer in my invoicing tool" and "create a draft invoice." Keep the list short.
  3. The service gives you a connector URL. In Claude, go to Customize, then Connectors, then Add custom connector, paste the URL and sign in. It's the same steps people use to add Scout.
  4. Test it with a fake customer or a test record first.

Not sure which actions to turn on? Ask Claude.

Copy this inI'm connecting [tool name] to Claude through [Zapier or Make]. These are the tasks I want help with: [tasks from your Step 2 table]. List the smallest set of actions I should allow, starting with read-only ones. For each, explain in one sentence what it lets Claude do, and flag any action that could send, charge or delete something so I keep it on "ask" or leave it off.

Automation services often charge for their plans or count each action as a task, so check their pricing before you rely on it.

6

Step 6: Level 3, build a connector for your own business

This is what I did with Scout Jobs. If your business has its own data or service (a booking system, a price list, a customer portal, a database), you can give it a connector of its own. Then you, your team, and even your customers can add it to Claude the same way people add Scout.

You don't have to write the code yourself. Claude Code is Anthropic's coding tool, and it can build this with you step by step, or you can hand this prompt to a developer.

Copy this inI want to build a custom MCP connector so Claude can use my business's data and actions. I'm not a developer, so explain each step in plain words before you do it, and wait for my OK. My business: [what you do, in one or two sentences] Where my data lives today: [for example: a Google Sheet of prices, an Airtable base of jobs, my booking software's API, a database] What Claude should be able to READ: [for example: look up a customer, check open time slots, get the price for a service] What Claude should be able to DO: [for example: create a draft quote, request a booking] Who will use it: [just me / my team / my customers] Please: 1. Recommend the simplest way to build this as a remote MCP server that Claude can add as a custom connector by URL, and where to host it. Tell me what it will cost to run. 2. Start with read-only tools only. Add actions later, one at a time. 3. Add sign-in so only the right people can use it, and make sure each person only sees their own data. 4. Keep every password, API key and secret in environment variables, never in the code or in this chat. 5. Give every tool a clear name and a plain-English description, so Claude knows when to use it. 6. Build it, test it locally with fake data, and show me how to check it works before anything goes live. 7. Give me the exact steps to add it in Claude under Customize, then Connectors, then Add custom connector. 8. Write a short checklist of what could go wrong and how we guard against it.

Go one tool at a time. A connector that does three things well beats one that does twenty things badly.

7

Step 7: A worked example, and the rules to keep

A quick example. Say you own a small plumbing company.

  • Level 1: connect Gmail and Google Calendar. Every morning: "List new quote requests from yesterday, draft a reply to each, and suggest two open times for a site visit." The owner reads the drafts and sends them.
  • Level 2: the company's job software isn't in Claude's directory, so it's connected through Zapier with two allowed actions: "find a job by customer name" and "add a note to a job." Now: "What's the status of the Garcia job, and add a note that they called."
  • Level 3: the price list and service area live in a spreadsheet. Claude Code helps build a small read-only connector with two tools: "get the price range for a service" and "check if an address is in our area." The office team adds it to Claude and answers quote calls faster.

The rules:

  • Start read-only. Add actions one at a time, after you trust it.
  • Keep "ask before using" on for anything that sends, publishes, pays or deletes.
  • Only connect what you need. Fewer tools means fewer mistakes.
  • Test with fake data and a test record before real customers.
  • Never paste passwords, API keys or customer card numbers into a chat.
  • Only connect services you trust, and remove connectors you stop using.
  • If customers will use your connector, make sure they can only see their own data, and tell them clearly what it does.
  • Check Claude's help center for the current plan rules before you plan around them.
