Meet Scout's AI team: the org chart of AI agents that runs a job search, and how to connect it to Claude

I built a team of AI agents that runs a job search. It's called Scout. You connect it to Claude, and from then on you do two things: upload your resume and say what job you want. The team does the rest.

It reads your career, figures out why you're not getting interviews, and builds you a Showcase: a personal website that sells you for the role you want. Then it hunts jobs across the major job boards and company career sites, keeps only the ones worth your time, builds a version of your Showcase for each job, applies for you if you turn that on, finds the hiring manager, and drafts a personal note with your Showcase link.

This guide shows you the whole team: Scout at the top, 35 AI managers that each own one outcome, and the 154 specialists who do the work underneath them, each with one clear job. Then it walks you through connecting Scout to Claude, step by step, so you can put the team to work on your own search.

One honest note: Scout prepares, you approve. Nothing gets sent, published or charged without your own tap. The only thing that runs on its own is Auto-Apply, and only inside rules you set.

Meet Scout's AI team: the org chart

Here's how the team is organized. You talk to one agent, Scout. Scout hands the work to a manager. Each manager owns one outcome, said the way you'd say it, and runs a small team of specialists who each do one thing well. The managers are grouped by the nine steps of a job search, in order.

4

Build a team like this for your business

You don't need 35 managers to get value from this. The pattern is what matters, and it works for any business:

1. One front agentThe person only ever talks to one agent. It knows them and routes the work.
2. Managers that own outcomesEach manager owns one result, named the way the customer would ask for it.
3. Specialists with one job eachSmall, narrow jobs are easier to get right and easier to fix.
4. A checker that validates claimsOne agent's whole job is to check the work against what's true before it ships.
5. A human approval gateNothing sends, publishes or charges until a person taps yes.

Start with one outcome your customers ask for most. Give it a manager, three or four specialists, and a checker. Add the next outcome only when that one works.
